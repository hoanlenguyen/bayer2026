# Design decisions

Read this before changing or adding a screen.

## Brief
- **Product:** recommends what to do with an analytics request: reuse, consolidate, migrate, retire or build new, with evidence. It builds nothing itself.
- **Users:** non-technical plant staff in any department, plus report owners who approve changes.
- **Main jobs:** get a trustworthy answer to "do we already have this?", see the recommended action with its evidence, check whether an asset can be trusted.
- **Platform:** desktop first; everything must still work at 375px.

## Look
- **Face:** Clean: white sidebar, `#F7F7F7` content area. On Ask and Explorer, cards and panels use the same `#F7F7F7` with a hairline border and dark text, to match the knowledge graph; warm grey is only for hints and switched-off controls. Shadows only on floating layers (search results).
- **Colours:** every page uses the knowledge graph's palette, from the tokens in `frontend/src/styles.css`. Warm charcoal `#3C3835` for text and `--accent`, warm grey `#6B6360` for secondary text and all graph dots, warm-tinted neutrals for lines and surfaces. Coral means selected or hovered and amber means linked, in both graphs. Darker coral, amber and a green carry status (`--bad`, `--warn`, `--ok`), so text stays readable. Item types are never told apart by colour: they use labels and letters on the dot. The one exception is recommendation outcomes: each has its own chip colour (`RecommendationChip`, `--rec-*` tokens), always with its name in the chip, so colour is never the only signal.
- **Font:** IBM Plex Sans; IBM Plex Mono only for IDs, code and score values. Both ship with the app (`@fontsource`, imported in `frontend/src/main.tsx`), so they need no outside network. Sidebar links change page without a reload, so the fonts and sidebar never redraw.
- **Themes:** light only for now: the `#F7F7F7` background and `#3C3835` text apply even when the system is in dark mode. The dark tokens remain in `styles.css` and apply only with `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>`. Never hard-code a colour.
- **Corners:** 12px for cards and controls 40px or taller, 8px below that, 24px for the composer and chat bubbles.

## Screens
- **History:** under a line in the sidebar, newest first, from the backend, so it follows the conversation and not the browser. Picking one brings back its messages, steps and recommendation, and sending next continues it, including an open follow-up. Reloading reopens the conversation that was open; "New question" starts fresh. Each entry has a delete button on hover or focus, which asks first; it is hidden while that chat is answering. Hidden when the sidebar is collapsed and on phones. The Ask page stays mounted on other pages, so an answer keeps arriving.
- **Sidebar:** collapses to a 64px icon rail with tooltips, and remembers the choice in this browser. The rail keeps a short "Sample" tag so sample data stays labelled. Phones use the top bar instead, with no toggle.
- **Ask:** the verdict card is the answer. It holds the recommendation (reuse, consolidation, migration, retirement or new build), the three scores with their pass mark, and what is missing. It only recommends, so there is no approval step.
- **How this was decided:** the six steps and their evidence, in a panel that collapses to a 56px rail and remembers the choice. On narrow screens, where it stacks under the chat, it collapses to its title row. The card does not repeat the evidence. Plain language by default. Agent traces, Python rules, weights, thresholds and raw specs appear with the "Technical details" switch, so the demo can still show the mechanics.
- **Conversation simulation (`/simulator`):** under Ask in the sidebar. Laid out like Ask: chat on the left, the same "How this was decided" panel on the right. SinceAI on the left and Brainrot on the right, each sentence with what was decided underneath (label, how sure, ms, who decided); "Needs a decision" is navy and bold, "Not enough detail yet" is amber. AnalytIQ answers with a short sentence and the same verdict card as Ask (scores, flags, rules, Estate Explorer button). The panel shows the latest answer's six steps; "Show how this was decided" on an older answer switches to it. A row of figures under the title counts sentences, who decided, tokens, Jev cost and time. A warning shows when Jev is off. Nothing is saved; the page stays mounted like Ask.
- **Estate Explorer, details:** each fact appears once. Status is in the header; owner and trust are under Governance.
- **Estate Explorer, graph:** filters are one row above the canvas so the graph gets the width. The key sits under the canvas.
- **Knowledge graph (`/cala-graph`):** full-screen rotating 3D map of the live catalog, styled after the graph on cala.ai: one warm grey for all dots (`--kg-dot`), a small caption card at the bottom right. Items are grouped by domain (workspace): close together inside a domain, clearly apart from other domains, with each domain's name above its group. KPIs are shared across domains, so they form their own labelled group in the middle. Real links between domains are drawn lighter than links inside a domain (`--kg-line-far`) and don't affect the layout, so groups stay apart; hovering an item strengthens its own links. Hovering or tapping an item turns it coral (`--kg-dot-hover`), shows its name in a tooltip below it, turns its linked items amber (`--kg-dot-linked`), and pauses the rotation; the card adds its type, status and domain. Clicking an item keeps this highlight after the pointer leaves and opens a panel on the right listing its linked items by relationship, with a search box that filters them by name or type; clicking a row selects that item, so people can walk the chain from domain to model to report; clicking it again, clicking empty space or pressing Esc clears it, and dragging never pins. Only real items are drawn: `ILLUSTRATIVE_DOTS` in `KnowledgeGraph.tsx` is off. It needs the backend, and has no links into the Explorer. A search at the top left finds any item and selects it.

- **BI Portfolio (`/bi-estate`):** the BI department head's tracker, last link in the sidebar. A heading with a one-line purpose, the demo identity picker on the right ("Acting as", read only by default). A grid of finding counts that are also filter buttons (consolidation counts pairs, with the number of reports beside it). Search, filters and Clear filters in one wrapping row; sort, direction and an optional-columns menu (creator, created, model) above the table. Filters, sort and page live in the query string, so Back restores them. Each row: title and ID, scope, accountable owner, imported use kept apart from AnalytIQ opens, finding tags and one plain sentence. Below 720px the table becomes one card per asset. Row details (`/bi-estate/<id>`) and the side-by-side comparison (`/bi-estate/compare/<a>/<b>`) are shareable addresses with a "Back to the portfolio" link that keeps the filters. The owner review sits under the comparison: status chip, both owners' responses, the form for whoever may act, Copy discussion brief, history. Every decision says it is recorded only.
- **Product name:** AnalytIQ intelligence in titles and the sidebar; "AnalytIQ" inside sentences.

## Rules
- Every decision shows its scores (see `CLAUDE.md`).
- One primary button per view: the action the screen exists for.
- No avatar on assistant messages; the user's bubble is enough to tell the two apart.
