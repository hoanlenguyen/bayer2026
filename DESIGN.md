# Design decisions

Read this before changing or adding a screen.

## Brief
- **Product:** recommends what to do with an analytics request: reuse, consolidate, migrate, retire or build new, with evidence. It builds nothing itself.
- **Users:** non-technical plant staff in any department, plus report owners who approve changes.
- **Main jobs:** get a trustworthy answer to "do we already have this?", see the recommended action with its evidence, check whether an asset can be trusted.
- **Platform:** desktop first; everything must still work at 375px.

## Look
- **Face:** Clean: white sidebar, `#F7F7F7` content area. On Ask and Explorer, cards and panels use the same `#F7F7F7` with a hairline border and dark text, to match the knowledge graph; warm grey is only for hints and switched-off controls. Shadows only on floating layers (search results).
- **Colours:** every page uses the knowledge graph's palette, from the tokens in `frontend/src/styles.css`. Warm charcoal `#3C3835` for text and `--accent`, warm grey `#6B6360` for secondary text and all graph dots, warm-tinted neutrals for lines and surfaces. Coral means selected or hovered and amber means linked, in both graphs. Darker coral, amber and a green carry status (`--bad`, `--warn`, `--ok`), so text stays readable. Item types are never told apart by colour: they use labels and letters on the dot. The one exception is recommendation outcomes: each has its own chip colour (`RecommendationChip`, `--rec-*` tokens), always with its name in the chip, so colour is never the only signal.
- **Font:** IBM Plex Sans; IBM Plex Mono only for IDs, code and score values. Both ship with the app (`@fontsource`, imported in `frontend/src/main.tsx`), so they need no outside network. Sidebar links change page without a reload, so the fonts and sidebar never redraw.
- **Themes:** light only for now: the `#F7F7F7` background and `#3C3835` text apply even when the system is in dark mode. The dark tokens remain in `styles.css` and apply only with `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>`. Never hard-code a colour.
- **Corners:** 12px for cards and controls 40px or taller, 8px below that, 24px for the composer and chat bubbles.

## Screens
- **History:** "History for this tab", under a line in the sidebar, newest first. It lists only the conversations this browser tab started (kept in `sessionStorage`; the transcripts stay in the backend). A conversation joins it when the server confirms it saved the first accepted message; a rejected-only attempt never appears, and a follow-up keeps its row at the top. A tab opened on its own, another browser or another profile starts empty; refreshing or moving around the app keeps the list and the open chat. Picking one brings back its messages, steps and recommendation, and sending next continues it, including an open follow-up. "New question" clears the screen, keeps every row and focuses the empty composer. Each entry has a delete button on hover or focus, which asks first; it is hidden while that chat is answering. Empty text: "Conversations from this tab will appear here." Hidden when the sidebar is collapsed and on phones. The Ask page stays mounted on other pages, so an answer keeps arriving. Ask has no saved-dashboards list: a dashboard is reached from the result that built or matched it, its own link, and the BI Portfolio and briefings.
- **Sidebar:** shows the AnalytIQ Intelligence logo at the top (40px tall; 32px in the phone top bar). Where it doesn't fit (collapsed rail, phones under 560px) the small square mark stands in. It collapses to a 64px icon rail with tooltips, and remembers the choice in this browser. At the bottom it says "Synthetic data by SinceAI" (hidden in the collapsed rail and the phone top bar). Phones use the top bar instead, with no toggle.
- **Ask:** the verdict card is the answer. It holds the recommendation (reuse, consolidation, migration, retirement or new build), the three scores with their pass mark, and what is missing. It only recommends, so there is no approval step.
- **How this was decided:** the six steps and their evidence, in a panel that collapses to a 56px rail and remembers the choice. On narrow screens, where it stacks under the chat, it collapses to its title row. The card does not repeat the evidence. Plain language by default. Agent traces, Python rules, weights, thresholds and raw specs appear with the "Technical details" switch, so the demo can still show the mechanics.
- **Conversation simulation (`/simulator`):** last in the sidebar, under BI Portfolio. Laid out like Ask: chat on the left, the same "How this was decided" panel on the right. SinceAI on the left and Brainrot on the right, each sentence with what was decided underneath (label, how sure, ms, who decided); "Needs a decision" is navy and bold, "Not enough detail yet" is amber. AnalytIQ answers with a short sentence and the same verdict card as Ask (scores, flags, rules, Estate Explorer button). The panel shows the latest answer's six steps; "Show how this was decided" on an older answer switches to it. A row of figures under the title counts sentences, who decided, tokens, Jev cost and time. A warning shows when Jev is off. Nothing is saved; the page stays mounted like Ask.
- **Estate Explorer, details:** each fact appears once. Status is in the header; owner and trust are under Governance.
- **Estate Explorer, graph:** filters are one row above the canvas so the graph gets the width. The key sits under the canvas.
- **Landing (`/introduction`, `/index` redirects there):** one screen, no sidebar. The AnalytIQ Intelligence logo (`frontend/src/assets/analytiq-logo.png`, transparent PNG) sits above the kicker line, 130px tall (80px on phones), and logo and text are centred together in the height of the screen. Text and the two buttons ("Try our solution" primary, opens Ask; "See the problem we solve" opens a dialog with the four problems, numbered, what AnalytIQ does about them a sentence under the list with the toggle inside it ("Hit [Solution rationale] to see how AnalytIQ Intelligence solves each problem."), and the same "Try our solution" button at the bottom. The toggle: the dialog title is struck through too and "AnalytIQ Intelligence solves it: …" appears under it in the same title style (the small label above becomes "How we solve it"); each problem's number turns into a red X, the problem is struck through, and its answer appears under it on a green band with a tick, one after another; closed with the X, Esc or a click outside) on the left (half the width), the live knowledge graph on the right (the other half) (same component as `/cala-graph`, dots only: no domain names, caption or search. Hovering an item lights up the item and its links, with no name shown, and clicks do nothing. Random items keep lighting up, up to two at once and started about 1 to 2 seconds apart: each fades in, its links grow out to its linked items with a small coral pulse running along each and a ring spreading from it, then it fades out after about 6 seconds. The links take 1.5 s to grow, so the movement stays calm. The first two to light up are the items nearest the centre, 0.3 s after load and 0.25 s apart, and their links grow about twice as fast; after that they are random. The hovered item animates the same way. A second style, where domain groups take turns fading away and coming back (`clusterCycle` on `KnowledgeGraph`), is built but switched off. The graph is sized to fill 95% of its panel, inside the page padding on any screen. Its dots are 1.6 times the full graph's size. Drag to turn; no zoom and no panning). Same colours as the other pages. A divider and "Built by Brainrot team" on the right at the bottom. Below 900px the graph stacks under the text. On load the text rises in one line after another, the graph grows in; buttons lift slightly on hover. None of this runs when the system asks for reduced motion.
- **Knowledge graph (`/cala-graph`):** full-screen rotating 3D map of the live catalog, styled after the graph on cala.ai: one warm grey for all dots (`--kg-dot`), a small caption card at the bottom right. Items are grouped by domain (workspace): close together inside a domain, clearly apart from other domains, with each domain's name above its group. KPIs are shared across domains, so they form their own labelled group in the middle. Real links between domains are drawn lighter than links inside a domain (`--kg-line-far`) and don't affect the layout, so groups stay apart; hovering an item strengthens its own links. Hovering or tapping an item turns it coral (`--kg-dot-hover`), shows its name in a tooltip below it, turns its linked items amber (`--kg-dot-linked`), and pauses the rotation; the card adds its type, status and domain. Clicking an item keeps this highlight after the pointer leaves and opens a panel on the right listing its linked items by relationship, with a search box that filters them by name or type; clicking a row selects that item, so people can walk the chain from domain to model to report; clicking it again, clicking empty space or pressing Esc clears it, and dragging never pins. Only real items are drawn: `ILLUSTRATIVE_DOTS` in `KnowledgeGraph.tsx` is off. It needs the backend, and has no links into the Explorer. A search at the top left finds any item and selects it.

- **Ask processing panel:** under the message just sent, a compact card with the same left accent: one status line with a spinner while real work runs (Checking the example scope… / Reading your request… / Checking your answer… / Checking workbook periods… / Checking the catalog… / Checking your dashboard details…), then, once the response is in, a bold label with one or two confirmed facts (Example brief, Your answer, Request received, Checks complete, Your selection). The next question with its choices, or the verdict and its next action, appear together about 1.5 s after sending, or as soon as slower work finishes. No indicator while Ask waits for the visitor. Rejections and errors show at once.
- **Dashboard build hand-off:** replaces the reviewed brief and Build button inside the Ask result card, same left accent. Heading "How your dashboard is prepared" (focused on approval), the status right under it (Checking and saving your dashboard… with a spinner, then Dashboard ready or Matched a saved dashboard with "Take a moment to review what went into it.", or the error and its recovery button), then "From your reviewed brief" and four numbered rows on a thin connector: Approved scope and period, Workbook coverage and source rows, Governed measures and view, Saved result. No countdown, no Open now, no timed highlight. If opening by itself was cancelled, an Open saved dashboard button appears under the status. The saved dashboard repeats a short receipt under its title.
- **Guided examples (Ask start screen):** two example buttons under the composer, three more in "More example questions" (a native disclosure, keyboard operable). Answers are buttons only, each choice with its resolved detail under the label (the period choices show the actual months). After the second answer the verdict card opens with "Review your dashboard brief" already showing: labelled lines (Purpose, Product, KPIs with ID and unit and the lead metric, Months, Source, Visuals) and a separate Build dashboard button. Nothing is saved before Build.
- **Country comparison dashboard (guided results):** the title names the product and both countries. Under it: a summary card per KPI (lead first and outlined) with both countries' latest values and the change from the prior month (percentage points for percentages); a bar comparison of the lead KPI in the latest month; two trend panels, one per KPI with its own scale, both countries in each (country A solid line with circles, country B dashed with squares, so colour is never the only cue); a month inspector (month buttons, or arrow keys on a focused chart) with both countries' values, record type and the workbook row. The full values table sits closed below as evidence. Panels stack on narrow screens.
- **BI Portfolio (`/bi-estate`):** the BI department head's tracker, last link in the sidebar. A heading with a one-line purpose, the demo identity picker on the right ("Acting as", read only by default). A grid of finding counts that are also filter buttons (consolidation counts pairs, with the number of reports beside it). Search, filters and Clear filters in one wrapping row; sort, direction and an optional-columns menu (creator, created, model) above the table. Filters, sort and page live in the query string, so Back restores them. Each row: title and ID, scope, accountable owner, imported use kept apart from AnalytIQ opens, finding tags and one plain sentence. Below 720px the table becomes one card per asset. Row details (`/bi-estate/<id>`) and the side-by-side comparison (`/bi-estate/compare/<a>/<b>`) are shareable addresses with a "Back to the portfolio" link that keeps the filters. The owner review sits under the comparison: status chip, both owners' responses, the form for whoever may act, Copy discussion brief, history. Every decision says it is recorded only.
- **Evidence & governance:** a second tab beside Tracker on `/bi-estate`, and on every dashboard. The index has three tabs (Applicable policies, Risks, Evidence), a scope line naming the portfolio size and active filters, one search box, and a count of distinct affected dashboards. Every item carries an authority chip (Demo document, Challenge workbook, AnalytIQ check, Source observation, Owner statement) and its locator (sheet, row, record, workbook SHA). Results use words with an outline chip (Finding, Evidence missing, Assessed: no finding, Exception in force), never colour alone. Undetermined and not-applicable rules sit in closed disclosures. Raw rows are one click away. Item pages (evidence, risk, document, assessment) have their own addresses and a link back to the dashboard; Back restores tracker filters.
- **Product name:** AnalytIQ intelligence in titles and the sidebar; "AnalytIQ" inside sentences.

## Rules
- Every decision shows its scores (see `CLAUDE.md`).
- One primary button per view: the action the screen exists for.
- No avatar on assistant messages; the user's bubble is enough to tell the two apart.
